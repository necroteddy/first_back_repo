When your team worked in one office, your IT team could protect one network. Today, your employees work from home, coffee shops, hotels, and co-working spaces — each with their own connection, their own devices, and their own risks.

Remote work has expanded your attack surface dramatically. Every home router, every personal laptop, every public Wi-Fi connection is a potential entry point for attackers.

Risk 01

Home networks are not business networks

Your office network was likely set up by IT professionals with security in mind. Your employee's home router was set up by a family member following a quick-start guide — and probably has never been updated since.

Home networks are shared with smart TVs, gaming consoles, children's tablets, and visiting guests. Any of these can be an entry point that puts your business data at risk.

One compromised device on a home network can expose everything else connected to it — including the laptop your employee uses for work.

Risk 02

Personal devices blur the line

When employees use personal laptops or phones for work, they mix business data with personal apps, personal files, and personal browsing habits. You have little visibility into what else is on that device — and little control if something goes wrong.

If a personal device is lost, stolen, or infected with malware, your business data goes with it. And unlike company-owned equipment, you may have no legal right to remotely wipe or recover that data.

⚠ Business risk

Personal devices are rarely updated regularly, often lack business-grade security software, and may be used by other household members — dramatically increasing the risk of a breach.

Risk 03

Shadow IT — the tools you don't know about

When remote workers cannot easily access the tools they need, they find their own. Personal Dropbox accounts, free file-sharing apps, consumer messaging tools — all used to get work done faster, all completely outside your control.

This is called "shadow IT" — and it is one of the most common hidden risks in remote teams. Your data is sitting in systems you did not approve, cannot monitor, and cannot secure.

The fix

7 ways to protect your remote team today

You do not need to bring everyone back to the office. You need the right policies, tools, and habits in place — and a team that understands why they matter.

1

Require a VPN for all work activity

A Virtual Private Network encrypts your team's internet connection, making it much harder for attackers to intercept data — even on unsecured networks.

2

Issue company devices where possible

Company-owned devices can be configured, monitored, and wiped remotely. This gives you control that personal devices simply cannot offer.

3

Enable multi-factor authentication on everything

Require a second step — like a phone confirmation — to log into any business system. This stops most account takeover attempts instantly.

4

Provide approved tools — and enforce their use

Give your team business-grade alternatives to consumer apps. Make clear which tools are approved and which are off-limits for work purposes.

5

Keep all software updated automatically

Unpatched software is one of the easiest ways attackers get in. Enable automatic updates on all work devices and business applications.

6

Train your team regularly

Remote workers face more attacks, with less support around them. Regular, short training sessions keep security habits sharp and awareness high.

7

Create a clear reporting culture

Remote workers are less likely to report something suspicious if they fear blame. Make it easy and safe to flag concerns — early reports prevent major breaches.

Remote security checklist
  • ✓ VPN in use for all remote work activity
  • ✓ Multi-factor authentication enabled on all business accounts
  • ✓ Company devices issued to staff handling sensitive data
  • ✓ Approved tools list shared with all remote employees
  • ✓ Automatic software updates enabled across all devices
  • ✓ Regular security training scheduled for the whole team
  • ✓ Clear process for reporting suspicious activity

Remote work is here to stay. The businesses that thrive are those that treat remote security not as an afterthought — but as a core part of how their team operates every single day.
