Cybersecurity on a budget — maximum protection for minimum spend

Good cybersecurity does not require a large budget. The most effective controls are either free or cost very little — and the businesses that use them consistently are far better protected than those spending ten times as much on the wrong things.

CyberSense Editorial  ·  6 min read

One of the most persistent myths about cybersecurity is that it is expensive. That it requires specialist staff, enterprise software, and significant ongoing investment. For large organisations with complex infrastructure, that is sometimes true. For most small businesses, it is not.

The vast majority of successful cyberattacks exploit basic vulnerabilities — unpatched software, weak passwords, no multi-factor authentication, poor backups. Fixing these costs almost nothing. The challenge is not money. It is consistency.

Start with what is completely free

Before spending a penny, there is a substantial amount of highly effective security available at no cost. These are not compromises or second-best options — they are the same controls that form the foundation of security at organisations of every size.

  • Multi-factor authentication — built into Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, and almost every business tool. Enable it on every account. Free.
  • Automatic software updates — enabling automatic updates on all devices and applications closes the majority of exploitable vulnerabilities. Free.
  • NCSC Cyber Essentials guidance — a free, government-backed framework that covers the five most important security controls and significantly reduces your risk. Free.
  • Have I Been Pwned — check whether any email address associated with your business has appeared in a known data breach. Free.
  • Google Workspace or Microsoft 365 security features — both platforms include substantial built-in security tools — secure email, MFA, device management basics, audit logging — that most businesses never enable. Free within your existing subscription.
  • Bitwarden — a fully featured, open-source password manager with a free tier that covers most small business needs. Free.

The £800 baseline — what it buys and why it works

A typical small business of five to ten people can achieve solid baseline protection for approximately £800 per year. That breaks down roughly as: a password manager for the team (£180), endpoint protection on all devices (£240), offsite cloud backup (£60), and Cyber Essentials certification (£300). Everything else — MFA, updates, Cloudflare, NCSC guidance — is free.

That £800 addresses the five most common attack vectors: weak passwords, malware, credential theft, ransomware without backups, and missing basic security hygiene. It does not address every possible threat — but it closes the doors that the vast majority of attackers use.

The return on investment of the £800 baseline is extraordinary. A single ransomware incident costs on average £150,000. The probability of a successful attack drops dramatically with these controls in place. The maths are not close.

Prioritise ruthlessly — not everything matters equally

With a limited budget, prioritisation is everything. Not all security controls have equal impact. Focus first on the things that prevent the most common, most damaging attacks — and do those consistently before adding anything else.

The priority order for budget-constrained businesses:

  • First: Enable MFA on all accounts — email, cloud tools, finance platforms, remote access. This single control stops the majority of account takeover attacks at zero cost.
  • Second: Deploy a password manager across the team. Unique passwords everywhere. This addresses credential theft and reuse.
  • Third: Enable automatic updates on all devices and software. This closes the known vulnerability window that most attacks exploit.
  • Fourth: Set up automated offsite backups. This transforms a ransomware attack from a potential existential event into a recoverable incident.
  • Fifth: Install basic endpoint protection. Detects and blocks most common malware before it executes.
  • Sixth: Train your team. Short, regular awareness — phishing recognition, reporting procedures, basic hygiene. The human layer amplifies everything else.
⚠ Consistency beats sophistication

A business that consistently does six basic things well is more secure than one that has expensive tools deployed inconsistently. MFA that is enabled on most accounts but not all, backups that run sometimes but are never tested, software updates that are applied eventually — these gaps are what attackers find and exploit. Doing the basics completely is more valuable than doing complex things partially.

Budget security checklist

  • MFA enabled on all accounts — email, cloud tools, finance, remote access
  • Password manager deployed across the team — unique passwords everywhere
  • Automatic updates enabled on all devices and business software
  • Offsite automated backups in place and tested
  • Endpoint protection installed on all business devices
  • Domain checked on Have I Been Pwned — alerts set up
  • Cloudflare configured on business domain — email security enabled
  • NCSC Cyber Essentials guidance reviewed and implemented
  • Staff training scheduled — short and regular, not annual and forgotten
  • Incident reporting process communicated — staff know who to contact

Cybersecurity on a budget is not about compromise. It is about focus. The businesses that get the most from limited spending are those that understand which controls actually matter, implement them completely, and maintain them consistently. That approach — not a large budget — is what makes a business genuinely hard to attack.
