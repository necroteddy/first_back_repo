Cybersecurity compliance — GDPR, Cyber Essentials, and what the law actually requires

Cybersecurity compliance is not just about avoiding fines. The frameworks and regulations that apply to UK businesses exist because the controls they require genuinely reduce risk. Understanding what applies to your business — and what it actually asks of you — turns compliance from a burden into a roadmap.

CyberSense Editorial  ·  7 min read

The landscape of cybersecurity regulation and standards affecting UK businesses has expanded significantly in recent years. UK GDPR, the Network and Information Systems Regulations, Cyber Essentials, PCI DSS, sector-specific FCA requirements, and the incoming Product Security and Telecommunications Infrastructure Act — navigating this landscape requires understanding which frameworks apply to your organisation, what they require, and how they relate to each other.

The good news is that there is significant overlap between what different frameworks require. A business that genuinely implements Cyber Essentials and UK GDPR data protection obligations will find that it is also well on its way to meeting many other compliance requirements. Good security practice and compliance are largely the same thing.

The key frameworks and who they apply to

Framework 01
UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018
Applies to: every business that processes personal data — which means almost every UK business

UK GDPR requires that personal data is processed lawfully, transparently, and for specified purposes; stored securely with appropriate technical and organisational measures; not kept longer than necessary; and protected against unauthorised access, loss, or destruction. A data breach that puts individuals at risk must be reported to the ICO within 72 hours. Fines can reach £17.5 million or 4% of global annual turnover — whichever is higher.

Framework 02
Cyber Essentials
Applies to: mandatory for government contracts involving personal data; increasingly required by enterprise customers; recommended for all businesses

Government-backed scheme covering five control areas: firewalls, secure configuration, access control, malware protection, and patch management. Available in two tiers: Cyber Essentials (self-assessment) and Cyber Essentials Plus (independently verified). Certification costs approximately £300 for the basic scheme and signals to customers, insurers, and partners that you meet a recognised security baseline.

Framework 03
Network and Information Systems (NIS) Regulations
Applies to: operators of essential services (energy, water, transport, health, digital infrastructure) and relevant digital service providers

Requires appropriate technical and organisational measures to manage security risks to network and information systems, and prompt notification of incidents with significant impact. NIS2 — the updated EU directive — has significantly expanded scope, and UK equivalents are under development. Businesses in essential sectors should review their current obligations against both NIS and emerging NIS2-equivalent UK legislation.

Framework 04
PCI DSS (Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard)
Applies to: any business that accepts, processes, stores, or transmits payment card data

Twelve requirements covering network security, access control, vulnerability management, monitoring, and policy. Non-compliance can result in card scheme fines, increased transaction fees, and loss of the ability to accept card payments. Compliance level is determined by annual transaction volume; most small businesses qualify for self-assessment. Minimising PCI scope — using hosted payment pages so card data never touches your systems — significantly reduces the compliance burden.

Framework 05
FCA operational resilience and cybersecurity requirements
Applies to: FCA-authorised firms — financial advisers, mortgage brokers, insurance companies, payment service providers, and others

FCA-authorised firms must identify important business services, set impact tolerances for disruption, and demonstrate they can remain within those tolerances. Cyber incidents are explicitly in scope. The Senior Managers and Certification Regime (SMCR) makes named individuals personally accountable for operational resilience failures. The FCA has fined firms and individuals for inadequate cybersecurity.

Framework 06
ISO 27001
Applies to: voluntary, but increasingly required by enterprise customers in procurement processes

International standard for information security management systems (ISMS). More comprehensive than Cyber Essentials — covering risk management, asset management, supplier relationships, and a full policy framework. Certification requires independent audit. For businesses supplying larger organisations, ISO 27001 certification is becoming a baseline expectation in enterprise procurement.

UK GDPR — the most broadly applicable framework

Because almost every business handles some personal data — employee records, customer contact details, invoice information — UK GDPR applies to almost every UK business. The security obligations are contained in Article 32, which requires "appropriate technical and organisational measures" to protect personal data.

The ICO's guidance makes clear that appropriate measures include: encryption of personal data where appropriate, the ability to ensure ongoing confidentiality, integrity, and availability of processing systems, the ability to restore data following an incident, and a process for regularly testing and evaluating the effectiveness of security measures. These requirements map directly onto good security practice — there is no meaningful distinction between "what GDPR requires" and "what good security looks like" for data protection purposes.

⚠ The 72-hour notification clock starts immediately

Under UK GDPR, if a personal data breach is likely to result in a risk to individuals' rights and freedoms, the ICO must be notified within 72 hours of you becoming aware — not 72 hours after your investigation is complete. Notify promptly based on what you know, clearly marking what is still under investigation. Late notification is treated as an aggravating factor. The ICO's online notification form is available at ico.org.uk/report.

Cyber Essentials — why it matters beyond compliance

Cyber Essentials is the most accessible and practical starting point for any UK business's security compliance journey. Its five control areas address the vulnerabilities that the NCSC estimates are responsible for the majority of successful cyberattacks against UK organisations.

Beyond the direct security benefit, Cyber Essentials certification provides: eligibility for government contracts requiring it; a signal to customers and partners that you take security seriously; a basis for cyber insurance applications; and a documented baseline against which to measure improvement. The self-assessment version costs approximately £300 and typically takes a day or two of effort for a small team.

Cyber Essentials Plus — the independently verified tier — is increasingly specified in enterprise procurement requirements. If your business supplies larger organisations, checking whether your key customers require Cyber Essentials Plus is worth doing before investing in certification at the basic self-assessment level.

Where the frameworks overlap — and how to manage them efficiently

The good news for businesses facing multiple compliance obligations is that the controls required by different frameworks overlap substantially. MFA, patching, access control, encryption, and incident response planning are required by virtually every security framework — implementing them once satisfies multiple obligations simultaneously.

The most efficient approach is to build a single security programme against a primary framework — Cyber Essentials for most small businesses, ISO 27001 for those with enterprise customer requirements — and then identify the incremental requirements of each additional framework that applies. The gap between Cyber Essentials and UK GDPR Article 32 requirements is small. The gap between Cyber Essentials and PCI DSS is larger but manageable for businesses that have already implemented strong baseline controls.

Compliance checklist

  • UK GDPR obligations reviewed — lawful basis for processing, data subject rights, security measures
  • ICO registration completed if required (most businesses processing personal data must register)
  • 72-hour breach notification process documented and understood
  • Cyber Essentials self-assessment completed or in progress
  • Cyber Essentials Plus evaluated if enterprise customers require it
  • PCI DSS self-assessment questionnaire completed if card payments are taken
  • FCA operational resilience requirements reviewed if FCA-authorised
  • NIS Regulations assessed if operating in an essential services sector
  • ISO 27001 evaluated if enterprise procurement requires it
  • Data Processing Agreements in place with all suppliers handling personal data
  • Records of processing activities maintained (required under UK GDPR)
  • Privacy policy current and accessible on your website

Compliance is not a substitute for security — a business can be compliant and insecure, or secure and non-compliant. But for most small businesses, the frameworks that apply to them are well-designed, practically achievable, and genuinely effective at reducing risk. Treating them as a roadmap rather than a burden is both more accurate and more useful.
